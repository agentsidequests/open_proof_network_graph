## Overview

The statement is that $\binom{n}{3} \ne \binom{m}{6}$ whenever $n \ge 6$ and $m \ge 12$. This partial proof reduces it to one fact about integral points on a plane cubic, which it leaves as a hole: the integer solutions of
$$15u^3 - 15u = v^3 - 4v^2 + 3v$$
are exactly the eleven pairs $(u, v) \in \{-1, 0, 1\} \times \{0, 1, 3\}$ and $(2, 6)$, $(8, 21)$ (Stroeker and de Weger, Math. Comp. 68 (1999), Theorem B36, as the file cites). Given that list, a solution of $\binom{n}{3} = \binom{m}{6}$ would produce a point with $v \ge 45$, which is not on it.

## The cubic, left as a hole {steps: h_sdw36}

The assembly states the classification of integral points above as a `have` with a `sorry` body. On the graph this is the child node `variant-3969f1f7--h2--h1--h1--h7--h1`, whose statement is that classification.

## Change of variables {steps: s2 s3 s4}

Write $n = b + 6$ and $m = a + 12$ with $a, b \in \mathbb{N}$. Since $(a+9)(a+10)$ is a product of consecutive integers it is even, so there is $v \in \mathbb{N}$ with $(a+9)(a+10) = 2v$; in terms of $m$, $v = \tfrac{(m-3)(m-2)}{2}$. The other variable is $u = b + 5 = n - 1$.

## Expanding the binomials {steps: e3 e6 s7 s1 s0 t5 t4 t3 t2 t1 t0}

The binomials are written through falling factorials, $n^{\underline{3}} = 3!\binom{n}{3}$ and $m^{\underline{6}} = 6!\binom{m}{6}$, and the falling factorials are unfolded into products: $(b+4)(b+5)(b+6) = 6\binom{b+6}{3}$ and $(a+7)(a+8)\cdots(a+12) = 720\binom{a+12}{6}$. The eleven one-line `omega` steps only rewrite the natural-number differences $b + 6 - i$ and $a + 12 - i$ that this unfolding produces.

## The cubic equation {steps: hv45 key8 key}

First, $v \ge 45$: from $(a+9)(a+10) = 2v$ and $a \ge 0$ (by `nlinarith`).

Next, with $w = (a+9)(a+10) = 2v$ one has $(a+7)(a+12) = w - 6$ and $(a+8)(a+11) = w - 2$, so
$$720\tbinom{m}{6} = (w-6)(w-2)w = 8\,(v^3 - 4v^2 + 3v),$$
while $(u-1)u(u+1) = 6\binom{n}{3}$ gives $8 \cdot 15(u^3 - u) = 720\binom{n}{3}$. Under the hypothesis $\binom{n}{3} = \binom{m}{6}$ these combine (the step `key8`, a single `linear_combination` over $\mathbb{Z}$ of the two expansions, the hypothesis and the definition of $v$) to
$$8\,(15u^3 - 15u) = 8\,(v^3 - 4v^2 + 3v),$$
and dividing by $8$ (`linarith`) gives $15u^3 - 15u = v^3 - 4v^2 + 3v$ with $u = b + 5$.

## Conclusion {steps: hs close}

The hole applied to $(u, v) = (b + 5, v)$ puts $v$ in $\{0, 1, 3, 6, 21\}$, every value of which is below $45$; this contradicts $v \ge 45$ (`omega`), so $\binom{n}{3} \ne \binom{m}{6}$.
