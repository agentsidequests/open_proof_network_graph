## Overview

Write $R(v) = \{\, n : \exists k \ge 1,\ 2k \le n,\ \binom{n}{k} = v \,\}$. The theorem says that for every $N$ some $v \ge N$ has $|R(v)| \ge 3$. The proof uses Singmaster's family in the following form. Let $a, b \in \mathbb{N}$ satisfy
$$b^2 = ab + a^2 + 1, \qquad (\ast)$$
put $c = a + 2b$, $n = (a+b)c$, $k = ac$, and $v = \binom{n}{k}$. Then $\binom{n}{k} = \binom{n-1}{k+1}$, so $v$ lies in the rows $n - 1$, $n$ and $v$ (the last from $\binom{v}{1}$). Consecutive Fibonacci numbers $a = F_{2i}$, $b = F_{2i+1}$ satisfy $(\ast)$, and then $n = F_{2i+2}F_{2i+3}$, $k = F_{2i}F_{2i+3}$; the proof does not name Fibonacci numbers but generates the solutions of $(\ast)$ directly. Three private lemmas precede the theorem: monotonicity of $\binom{n}{j}$ in $j$ on $j \le n/2$ (`e849f_choose_mono_right`, induction on $j$ from Mathlib's `Nat.choose_le_succ_of_lt_half_left`), its consequence $n \le \binom{n}{k}$ for $1 \le k$, $2k \le n$ (`e849f_le_choose`), and the three lemmas described in the sections below.

## Solutions of the Pell-type equation {steps: s1 hb}

**Lemma (`e849f_pairs`).** For every $N$ there are $a, b$ with $a \ge N + 1$ satisfying $(\ast)$. By induction on $N$: $(a, b) = (1, 2)$ starts it, and if $(a, b)$ satisfies $(\ast)$ then so does $(a + b,\ a + 2b)$, since $(a+2b)^2 - (a+b)(a+2b) - (a+b)^2 = b^2 - ab - a^2$ (checked by `nlinarith` from $(\ast)$); also $a + b \ge a + 1$ because $(\ast)$ forces $b \ge 1$. The theorem takes such a pair with $a \ge N + 1$ (step `s1`), and $a < b$ follows from $(\ast)$ (`hb`, by `nlinarith`).

## The polynomial identity {steps: hpoly hbc hnk hc5 hk5 hgap hbca s10 hkm hrel}

**Lemma (`e849f_poly`).** Under $(\ast)$,
$$(a+b)c\,(ac+1) + bc = bc \cdot bc .$$
Over $\mathbb{Z}$ the difference of the two sides is $-c^2\,(b^2 - ab - a^2 - 1)$, so the identity is `linear_combination` $(-c^2)\cdot(\ast)$, cast back to $\mathbb{N}$.

With $n = (a+b)c$ and $k = ac$ one has $n = k + bc$ (`hnk`), $bc \ge 1$, $c \ge 5$, $k \ge 5$, and $(b - a)c \ge 3$ (from $b > a$ and $c \ge 5$). Writing $n = m + 1$ (`s10`), $k < m$, and the identity becomes, since $m + 1 - k = bc$ and $m - k = bc - 1$,
$$(m+1)(k+1) = (m-k)(m+1-k) \qquad (\text{step } \texttt{hrel}).$$

## The coincidence of binomials {steps: hchoose}

**Lemma (`e849f_choose_eq`).** If $k < m$ and $(m+1)(k+1) = (m-k)(m+1-k)$, then $\binom{m+1}{k} = \binom{m}{k+1}$. Mathlib's identities $\binom{m}{k}(m+1) = \binom{m+1}{k}(m+1-k)$ and $\binom{m}{k+1}(k+1) = \binom{m}{k}(m-k)$ give
$$\tbinom{m+1}{k}(m+1-k)(k+1) = \tbinom{m}{k}(m+1)(k+1) = \tbinom{m}{k}(m-k)(m+1-k) = \tbinom{m}{k+1}(k+1)(m+1-k),$$
and the positive factor $(m+1-k)(k+1)$ cancels. Hence $v = \binom{n}{k} = \binom{n-1}{k+1}$.

## Three rows {steps: hn2 hvn hna hsub hfin h3 close}

The value $v$ exceeds $n$: $2k \le n$ (from $n = k + bc$ and $bc \ge ac + 3$), so monotonicity gives $\binom{n}{2} \le \binom{n}{k} = v$, and $2\binom{n}{2} = n(n-1) \ge 4n$ because $n \ge 5$, so $v \ge 2n > n$ (`hvn`). Since $a \le n$, also $v > n \ge a > N$, which is the bound $v \ge N$.

The three numbers $n - 1 < n < v$ are distinct and all lie in $R(v)$ (`hsub`): $n - 1$ with $k + 1$, where $2(k+1) \le n - 1$ because $(b-a)c \ge 3$; $n$ with $k$; and $v$ with $1$, as $2 \le v$. $R(v)$ is finite, since each of its rows $x$ has $x \le \binom{x}{j} = v$ (`hfin`, by `e849f_le_choose`). A finite set containing a three-element set has `Set.ncard` at least $3$, which closes the proof.
