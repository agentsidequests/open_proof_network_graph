$$\forall n \in \mathbb{N},\ 1092 \le n \ \Rightarrow\ \exists q \in \mathbb{N}:\ q \text{ prime} \ \wedge\ \Big( \big(n < 2q-2 \ \wedge\ 2q+8 \le 2n \ \wedge\ 2q-1 \text{ prime}\big) \ \vee\ \big(n < 4q-2 \ \wedge\ 4q+8 \le 2n \ \wedge\ 4q-1 \text{ prime}\big) \Big)$$

Lean conventions: `2*q - 2`, `2*q - 1`, `4*q - 2`, `4*q - 1` are natural-number subtractions (0 when the right side is larger); since $q$ is prime, $q \ge 2$ and they are exact. The bound $2q+8 \le 2n$ is $2q-2 \le 2n-10$ written without subtraction, and $4q+8 \le 2n$ is $4q-2 \le 2n-10$.

In words: for every $n \ge 1092$ the interval $(n,\, 2n-10]$ contains a number $m$ of the form $2q-2$ or $4q-2$, with $q$ prime, such that $m+1$ is prime as well. Equivalently, there is a prime $q$ with $2q-1$ prime and $n < 2q-2 \le 2n-10$, or a prime $q$ with $4q-1$ prime and $n < 4q-2 \le 2n-10$.
