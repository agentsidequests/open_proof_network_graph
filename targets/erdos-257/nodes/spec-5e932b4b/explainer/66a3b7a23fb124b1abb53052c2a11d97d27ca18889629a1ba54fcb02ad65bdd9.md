## The argument

Fix $j$ and write $c(N) = \#\{a \in \mathbb{N} : j < a < m,\ a \mid N\}$ and $G_j(m) = \sum_{i \ge 0} 2^{-(i+1)} c(m+i+1)$. Fix $m \ge 4j^2 + 1092$. If $m+1$ is prime and $(m+2)/2$ is prime, or $4 \mid m+2$ and $(m+2)/4$ is prime, the first disjunct holds. Otherwise we show $G_j(m) > 99/100$, in two cases: $m+1$ composite, or $m+1$ prime with the condition on $m+2$ failing.

**Truncation.** Each set counted by $c(m+i+1)$ lies in $\{0, \dots, m-1\}$, so $c(m+i+1) \le m$ and the series is dominated by $\sum_i m\, 2^{-(i+1)}$. Its terms are nonnegative, so for every $I$, $G_j(m) \ge \sum_{i < I} 2^{-(i+1)} c(m+i+1)$.

**Small divisors.** For $m < N \le m + 12$ and $D \le 12$, $c(N) \ge s_D(N) := \#\{d : 2 \le d \le D,\ d \mid N\}$. The map $d \mapsto N/d$ is injective on the divisors $d$ of $N$, and it sends them into $(j, m)$: writing $N = dk$, $12k \ge dk = N > m \ge 4j^2 + 1092 > 12j$ gives $k > j$, and $2k \le N \le m + 12$ gives $k < m$.

**A composite $m+1$.** If $m+1$ is not prime, let $p$ be its least prime factor and $a = (m+1)/p$. Then $p^2 \le m+1 = pa$, so $p \le a$ and $a^2 \ge m+1 > j^2$, whence $a > j$; and $a \le (m+1)/2 < m$. So $c(m+1) \ge 1$.

**Three divisors of $m+2$.** Suppose $m+1$ is prime and the condition on $m+2$ fails. As $m+1$ is an odd prime, $m+2 = 2u$ with $u \ge 547$ not prime. If $u$ is odd, its least prime factor $s$ is at least 3; write $u = sv$, so $s \le v$ and $v^2 \ge u$, whence $v \ge 24$ and, as $2u = m+2 > 4j^2$, $v > j$. The numbers $v < 2v < sv = u$ are three divisors of $m+2$ in $(j, m)$. If $u = 2t$, then $4 \mid m+2$ and $t = (m+2)/4 \ge 274$ is not prime; write $t = sw$ with $s$ its least prime factor, so $s \le w$ and $w^2 \ge t$. As $4t = m+2 > 4j^2$, $w^2 \ge t > j^2$ gives $w > j$. If $s = 2$ the divisors $w < t < 2t$ serve; if $s \ge 3$ then $2w < 3w \le t < 2t$ serve. In both cases $c(m+2) \ge 3$.

**Two finite checks.** $s_D(N)$ depends only on $N$ modulo $\operatorname{lcm}(2, \dots, D)$, which is $60$ for $D = 6$ and $27720$ for $D = 12$. The kernel evaluates (by `decide +kernel`):

- for every residue $r$ modulo $60$: $\tfrac12 \max(s_6(r+1), 1) + \sum_{i=1}^{7} 2^{-(i+1)} s_6(r+i+1) > \tfrac{99}{100}$;
- for every residue $r$ modulo $27720$ with $r+1$ prime to $2, 3, 5, 7, 11$: $\sum_{i=0}^{11} 2^{-(i+1)} b_i > \tfrac{99}{100}$, where $b_1 = \max(s_{12}(r+2), 3)$ and $b_i = s_{12}(r+i+1)$ for $i \ne 1$. The residues are checked in fourteen blocks of 2000. The smallest value is $4066/4096 \approx 0.99268$, at $r = 15400$.

**Conclusion.** If $m+1$ is composite, take $r = m \bmod 60$: by truncation at $I = 8$, the small-divisor bound with $D = 6$ and $c(m+1) \ge 1$, $G_j(m)$ is at least the first sum, which exceeds $99/100$. If $m+1$ is prime and the condition on $m+2$ fails, $m+1 > 11$ has none of $2, 3, 5, 7, 11$ as a factor; take $r = m \bmod 27720$: by truncation at $I = 12$, the small-divisor bound with $D = 12$ and $c(m+2) \ge 3$, $G_j(m)$ is at least the second sum, which exceeds $99/100$.
