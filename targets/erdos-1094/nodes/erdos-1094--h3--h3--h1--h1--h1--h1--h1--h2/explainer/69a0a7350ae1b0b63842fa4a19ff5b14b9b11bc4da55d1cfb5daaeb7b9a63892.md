## Overview

Write $S(N)=\sum_{0\le n<N} f(n)$ and, for a test function $\psi$, $T_\psi(X)=\sum_{n\ge 0} f(n)\,\psi(n/X)$. The hypotheses are: $f\ge 0$; $T_\psi(X)/X\to A\int_{\mathbb R}\psi$ as $X\to\infty$ for every $C^2$ function $\psi$ with compact support contained in $(0,\infty)$; and $S(N)\le CN$ for all $N$. The claim is $S(N)/N\to A$.

The proof sandwiches the sharp cut-off $\mathbf 1_{[0,1)}$ between two smooth bumps. Three lemmas are proved before the theorem.

- **Bumps.** For $c\in\mathbb R$ and $0<r<R$ there is a $C^2$ (indeed smooth) function $\psi$ with compact support contained in $(c-R,c+R)$, with $0\le\psi\le 1$, $\psi=1$ on $[c-r,c+r]$, and $2r\le\int\psi\le 2R$. It is Mathlib's smooth bump function on $\mathbb R$; the two integral bounds come from comparing $\psi$ with the indicator functions of $[c-r,c+r]$ and $(c-R,c+R)$.
- **Lower sum.** If $\psi\le 1$ everywhere and $\psi(x)=0$ for $x\ge 1$, then for $N\ge 1$ the series $T_\psi(N)$ has only the terms $n<N$, and each is at most $f(n)$; so $T_\psi(N)\le S(N)$.
- **Upper sum.** If $\psi\ge 0$, $\psi(x)=0$ for $x\ge 2$, $\psi=1$ on $[\delta,1]$, and $M=\lceil\delta N\rceil\le N$, then $S(N)\le S(M)+T_\psi(N)$: split the range $[0,N)$ at $M$; on $[M,N)$ one has $\delta\le n/N\le 1$, so $f(n)=f(n)\psi(n/N)$, and this finite sum is at most the whole of $T_\psi(N)$, which has only the terms $n<2N$, all non-negative.

## Choosing the constants {steps: s1 hC'nn hC' hD h_x3b4pos h_x3b41 h_x3b42 h_x3b43 h_x3b4A h_x3b4C}

Replace $C$ by $C'=\max(C,0)\ge 0$, so $S(N)\le C'N$ still holds. Given $\varepsilon>0$, put $\delta=\min\bigl(\tfrac12,\ \varepsilon/(8(|A|+C'+1))\bigr)$. Then $0<\delta\le\tfrac12$, and $|A|\delta\le\varepsilon/8$ and $C'\delta\le\varepsilon/8$.

## The two bumps {steps: s11 s12 upos wpos uzero wzero wone'}

Apply the bump lemma twice with $r=(1-\delta)/2$ and $R=1/2$: once with centre $1/2$, giving $u$ supported in $(0,1)$ and equal to $1$ on $[\delta/2,1-\delta/2]$; once with centre $(1+\delta)/2$, giving $w$ supported in $(\delta/2,1+\delta/2)$ and equal to $1$ on $[\delta,1]$. Both supports lie in $(0,\infty)$, $u(x)=0$ for $x\ge 1$, $w(x)=0$ for $x\ge 2$ (as $\delta\le\tfrac12$), and both integrals lie in $[1-\delta,1]$.

## Applying the hypothesis along the integers {steps: hut hwt s20 s21 s22}

The smoothed hypothesis for $u$ and $w$, composed with $N\mapsto N$ from $\mathbb N$ to $\mathbb R$ (which tends to infinity), gives $T_u(N)/N\to A\int u$ and $T_w(N)/N\to A\int w$ along the integers. Choose $N_1$, $N_2$ beyond which these are within $\varepsilon/4$ of their limits, and $N_3>8C'/\varepsilon$.

## The estimate for large N {steps: hNN1 hNN2 hNN3 hNN4 hNpos e1 e2 hM lo up hMb a1 a2 s36 s37 s38 s39 hlow hup hCN close}

Take $N\ge\max(N_1,N_2,N_3,1)$. Since $\delta<1$, $M=\lceil\delta N\rceil\le N$. The lower-sum lemma gives $T_u(N)\le S(N)$; the upper-sum lemma gives $S(N)\le S(M)+T_w(N)$, and $S(M)\le C'M\le C'(\delta N+1)$ because $\lceil x\rceil<x+1$. Since $\int u,\int w\in[1-\delta,1]$, $|A\int u-A|\le|A|\delta$ and $|A\int w-A|\le|A|\delta$. Dividing by $N$:
$$A-\tfrac{\varepsilon}{8}-\tfrac{\varepsilon}{4}<\frac{T_u(N)}{N}\le\frac{S(N)}{N}\le C'\delta+\frac{C'}{N}+\frac{T_w(N)}{N}<\tfrac{\varepsilon}{8}+\tfrac{\varepsilon}{8}+A+\tfrac{\varepsilon}{8}+\tfrac{\varepsilon}{4},$$
using $C'/N<\varepsilon/8$ from $N\ge N_3$. Both ends are within $\varepsilon$ of $A$, so $|S(N)/N-A|<\varepsilon$, which is the definition of $S(N)/N\to A$ (closed by linear arithmetic).
