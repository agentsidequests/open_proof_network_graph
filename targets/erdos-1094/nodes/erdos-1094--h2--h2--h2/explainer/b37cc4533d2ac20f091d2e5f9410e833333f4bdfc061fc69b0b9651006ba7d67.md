## Overview

Write $\theta=21/40$ and let $\mathrm{BHP}$ be the statement: there are $C>0$ and $k_0$ such that for every integer $k\ge k_0$ the open interval $(k,k+k^{\theta})$ contains at least $C\,k^{\theta}/\log k$ primes. For an integer $n$ and $k\in\mathbb N$ write $P_k(n)=\prod_{i=1}^{k}(n-i)$, and say that $(k,n)$ *has a good prime* if some prime $p$ with $k<p<k+3k^{\theta}$ divides $P_k(n)$ in $\mathbb Z$. The theorem is
$$\neg\mathrm{BHP}\ \lor\ \exists k_0\ \forall k\ge k_0\ \forall n\in\mathbb Z:\ 2k<n\le \exp\!\Bigl(\frac{\log^2 k}{20\log\log k}\Bigr)\ \Rightarrow\ (k,n)\text{ has a good prime}.$$
It is assembled from four statements of the same shape, $\neg\mathrm{BHP}\lor\exists k_0\,\forall k\ge k_0\,\forall n$ in a range, $(k,n)$ has a good prime, one for each of the four ranges
$$2k<n\le\tfrac12k^{2-\theta},\qquad \tfrac12k^{2-\theta}<n\le\frac{k^2}{\log^2k},\qquad \frac{k^2}{\log^2k}<n\le\tfrac12k^{2+\theta},\qquad \tfrac12k^{2+\theta}<n\le\exp\!\Bigl(\frac{\log^2 k}{20\log\log k}\Bigr).$$
These are the four range cases of van Doorn and Tang's main theorem; each is one of this node's four hole nodes, proved and merged, and their theorems are used here as they stand.

## The four range cases {steps: hall}

The conjunction of the four range statements is proved component by component: the small, medium, medium-large and large cases are exactly the theorems of this node's holes `--h1`, `--h2`, `--h3` and `--h4`, each applied as it stands.

## Taking the cases apart {steps: s2 s3 s4 s5 s6}

Split the conjunction into its four disjunctions. If any of them is the alternative $\neg\mathrm{BHP}$, the theorem holds by its first disjunct. Otherwise each case supplies a threshold $k_i$ ($i=1,\dots,4$) beyond which its range has good primes, and it remains to prove the second disjunct.

## Combining the thresholds {steps: hk1 hk2 hk3 hk4 s11 s12}

Take $k_0=\max(k_1,k_2,k_3,k_4)$, and let $k\ge k_0$ and $n\in\mathbb Z$ with $2k<n\le\exp(\log^2k/(20\log\log k))$. Then $k\ge k_i$ for each $i$. Compare $n$ successively with the three cut points $\tfrac12k^{2-\theta}$, $k^2/\log^2k$ and $\tfrac12k^{2+\theta}$: if $n\le\tfrac12k^{2-\theta}$ the small case applies (with the hypothesis $2k<n$); otherwise, if $n\le k^2/\log^2k$, the medium case; otherwise, if $n\le\tfrac12k^{2+\theta}$, the medium-large case; and otherwise the large case, whose upper bound is the hypothesis on $n$. In every branch the case gives a good prime for $(k,n)$.
