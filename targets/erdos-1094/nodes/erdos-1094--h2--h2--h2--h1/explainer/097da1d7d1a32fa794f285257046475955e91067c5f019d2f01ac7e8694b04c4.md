## Overview

Write $\theta=21/40$, $P_k(n)=\prod_{i=1}^{k}(n-i)$ for $n\in\mathbb Z$, and let $\pi(a,b)$ be the number of primes $p$ with $a<p<b$ (counted, as in the statement, over the integers $\lfloor a\rfloor<p<\lceil b\rceil$). Let $\mathrm{BHP}$ be: there are $C>0$ and $k_0$ with $C\,k^{\theta}/\log k\le\pi(k,k+k^{\theta})$ for all integers $k\ge k_0$. The theorem says: $\neg\mathrm{BHP}$, or there is $k_0$ such that for all $k\ge k_0$ and all integers $n$ with $2k<n\le\tfrac12k^{2-\theta}$ some prime $p$ with $k<p<k+3k^{\theta}$ divides $P_k(n)$.

The proof first replaces $21/40$ by a letter $\theta$ with $0<\theta<1$ and names the counting function $\pi(a,b)$, then proves eight auxiliary facts, and finally splits on whether $\mathrm{BHP}$ holds.

## The exponent {steps: h_x3b80 h_x3b81 s3}

$0<\theta<1$ is checked numerically for $\theta=21/40$, and from here on only these two facts about $\theta$ are used. The function $\pi(a,b)$ is introduced as the cardinality of the filtered integer interval.

## Counting primes in intervals {steps: primeCard_le_add exists_prime_of_primeCard_pos}

For $b\le c$: $\pi(a,c)\le\pi(a,b)+(c-b+1)$. A prime counted in $(a,c)$ either lies below $b$, and is then counted in $(a,b)$, or is an integer in $[\lceil b\rceil,\lceil c\rceil)$, a set of at most $c-b+1$ integers. And if $\pi(a,b)>0$, there is a prime $p$ with $a<p<b$.

## Asymptotic comparisons {steps: tendsto_log_rpow_mul_rpow_neg poly_log_lt}

For any $b$ and $c>0$, $(\log x)^b x^{-c}\to 0$ as $x\to\infty$ (Mathlib: a power of $\log$ is $o(x^c)$). Consequently, for $\alpha<t$ and $C>0$, eventually in $k\in\mathbb N$, $A\,k^{\alpha}(\log k)^{\beta}\le C\,k^{t}/\log k$, since the ratio of the two sides is a constant times $(\log k)^{\beta+1}k^{\alpha-t}\to 0$.

## The count beats the excess {steps: excess_le count_beats_excess}

If $1\le k\le N\le k+2k^{\theta}+1$ then $N^{\theta}-k^{\theta}\le (N-k)^{\theta}\le (3k^{\theta})^{\theta}=3^{\theta}k^{\theta^2}$, by subadditivity of $x\mapsto x^{\theta}$ and $k^{\theta}\ge 1$. Then for each $C>0$, eventually in $k$, for every such $N$,
$$N^{\theta}-k^{\theta}+2<C\,N^{\theta}/\log N.$$
Indeed, by the previous section (with exponents $\theta^2<\theta$ and $0<\theta$), eventually $3^{\theta}k^{\theta^2}+3\le \tfrac C2 k^{\theta}/\log k$; and for $k\ge 4$ one has $N\le 4k\le k^2$, so $\log N\le 2\log k$, $\log N>0$ and $k^{\theta}\le N^{\theta}$, which give $\tfrac C2k^{\theta}/\log k\le C N^{\theta}/\log N$.

## Divisors of the window product {steps: dvd_Pprod_of_factor dvd_Pprod_of_mem}

If $n-k<t<n$ and $p\mid t$, then $t=n-i$ with $1\le i\le k$ is a factor of $P_k(n)$, so $p\mid P_k(n)$.

## If BHP holds: a prime in every window {steps: s12.exists_prime_in_window s12.s2}

Assume $\mathrm{BHP}$ with constants $C,k_0$. There is a threshold such that for all $k\ge k_1$ and every real $a$ with $k\le a\le k+2k^{\theta}$ there is a prime $p$ with $a<p<a+k^{\theta}$. Take $k\ge\max(k_0,k_1,2)$, with $k_1$ the threshold of the excess bound for this $C$, and put $N=\lceil a\rceil$, so $k\le N\le k+2k^{\theta}+1$ and $N<a+1$. By $\mathrm{BHP}$ at $N$, $\pi(N,N+N^{\theta})\ge C N^{\theta}/\log N$. Since $a+k^{\theta}\le N+N^{\theta}$, the counting lemma gives
$$\pi(N,a+k^{\theta})\ge\pi(N,N+N^{\theta})-\bigl(N^{\theta}-k^{\theta}+2\bigr)>0$$
by the excess bound. So there is a prime $p$ with $N<p<a+k^{\theta}$, and $p>N\ge a$.

## If BHP holds: the two cases {steps: s12.hk2 s12.hkw s12.hk0 s12.hk_x3b8 s12.hN s12.hm s12.hm_ge2 s12.hm2 s12.hmk s12.hmk2 s12.hm_lt s12.hkey s12.hdiv s12.hdiv2 s12.s17 s12.s18}

Take $k$ at least the window threshold and $2$, and $2k<n\le\tfrac12k^{2-\theta}$. Let $m=\lfloor (n-1)/k\rfloor$, so $mk\le n-1<(m+1)k$ and $m\ge 2$. From $n\le\tfrac12k^{2-\theta}=\tfrac12k^{1-\theta}\cdot k$ we get $m<\tfrac12k^{1-\theta}$, hence $(2m-1)k^{\theta}<k$. Also $\tfrac m{m-1}\le 2$.

*Case $n<mk+mk^{\theta}$.* Apply the window lemma at $a=k+\tfrac m{m-1}k^{\theta}\in[k,k+2k^{\theta}]$: a prime $p$ with $a<p<a+k^{\theta}$. Then $k<p<k+3k^{\theta}$. Put $t=(m-1)p$. On one side $t>(m-1)k+mk^{\theta}=mk+mk^{\theta}-k>n-k$; on the other $t<(m-1)k+mk^{\theta}+(m-1)k^{\theta}=mk-k+(2m-1)k^{\theta}<mk\le n-1$. So $n-k<t<n$ and $p\mid t$, hence $p\mid P_k(n)$.

*Case $n\ge mk+mk^{\theta}$.* Apply the window lemma at $a=k$: a prime $p$ with $k<p<k+k^{\theta}$, so $p\ge k+1$. Put $t=mp$. Then $t\ge m(k+1)\ge mk+1>n-k$ (from $n-1<(m+1)k$), and $t<m(k+k^{\theta})\le n$. Again $p\mid P_k(n)$.

## If BHP fails {steps: s13}

If the counting form of $\mathrm{BHP}$ fails, so does $\mathrm{BHP}$ as stated (the two counts are the same number), which is the first disjunct.
