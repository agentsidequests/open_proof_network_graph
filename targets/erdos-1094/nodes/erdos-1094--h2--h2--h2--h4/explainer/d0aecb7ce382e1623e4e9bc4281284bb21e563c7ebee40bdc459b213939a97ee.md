## Overview

Write $\theta=21/40$, $P_k(n)=\prod_{i=1}^{k}(n-i)$, $\|x\|$ for the distance to a nearest integer, and $\pi(a,b)$ for the number of primes in $(a,b)$. Let $\mathrm{BHP}$ be: there are $C>0$ and $k_0$ with $Ck^{\theta}/\log k\le\pi(k,k+k^{\theta})$ for all $k\ge k_0$. The theorem: $\neg\mathrm{BHP}$, or eventually in $k$, for every integer $n$ with $\tfrac12k^{2+\theta}<n\le\exp\bigl(\log^2k/(20\log\log k)\bigr)$, some prime $p$ with $k<p<k+3k^{\theta}$ divides $P_k(n)$.

The scheme is the medium-case scheme. Let $B(k,n)$ be the integers $m$ with $k<m<k+k^{\theta}$ and $\|n/m\|<k^{\theta-1}$. If $\#B(k,n)<\pi(k,k+k^{\theta})$, a prime $p\in(k,k+k^{\theta})$ outside $B(k,n)$ has $1\le n\bmod p\le k$ and so divides $P_k(n)$. The difference here is that Konyagin's bound, the hole of the sibling node `--h3` used through its theorem, is applied with an exponent $r$ that grows with $k$. Here is the bound: for $\lambda\ge 1$, $k<n$ and $2\le r\le\tfrac12k^{1-\theta}$,
$$\#B(k,n)<c_6\,k^{\theta}\Bigl[\Bigl(\tfrac{n\,r!\,\lambda^r}{k^{r+1}}\Bigr)^{\frac1{2r-1}}+\Bigl(\tfrac{k^{r+\theta}}{n\,r!\,\lambda^r}\Bigr)^{\frac1{r-1}}+\Bigl(\tfrac{(r+1)\lambda}{k}\Bigr)^{\frac1{2r}}\Bigr]+2r\lambda,$$
with $c_6=65536$.

**The choice of $r$.** Put $r_0(k)=\lceil\log k/(10\log\log k)\rceil$. Eventually, $r_0\ge 1$, $3r_0-2<\tfrac7{20}\log k/\log\log k$, $r_0\le\tfrac3{20}\log k/\log\log k$, $r_0\le\log k$, $r_0\le\tfrac12k^{1-\theta}$ and $r_0+1\le k^{\theta/r_0}$. These follow from $\log k/\log\log k\to\infty$. On the range, $n\,r_0!\le k^{r_0+\theta}$; this is where the upper bound $n\le\exp(\log^2k/(20\log\log k))$ is used. Let $r$ be the *least* natural number with $n\,r!\le k^{r+\theta}$. Then $r\le r_0$, so $r$ satisfies the same size bounds. Minimality gives $k^{(r-1)+\theta}<n\,(r-1)!$. Finally $r\ge 3$, because $n>\tfrac12k^{2+\theta}$ excludes $r\le 2$.

**The choice of $\lambda$.** Put $E_1=(1-\theta)(2r-1)/(3r-2)$ and $\lambda=\bigl(k^{r+1-E_1}/(n\,r!)\bigr)^{1/r}$, so $\lambda^r=k^{r+1-E_1}/(n\,r!)$. From $n\,r!\le k^{r+\theta}$ we get $\lambda\ge 1$, and from minimality $\lambda<k^{(2-\theta)/r}$. With this $\lambda$ the first two terms in the bracket are both $k^{(\theta-1)/(3r-2)}$. So
$$\#B(k,n)<c_6k^{\theta}\bigl(2k^{(\theta-1)/(3r-2)}+((r+1)\lambda/k)^{1/(2r)}\bigr)+2r\lambda .$$

**The asymptotics.** Using $3r-2<\tfrac7{20}\log k/\log\log k$, one gets $k^{(\theta-1)/(3r-2)}\le(\log k)^{-19/14}$. Using $\lambda<k^{(2-\theta)/r}$, $r+1\le k^{\theta/r}$ and $r\le\tfrac3{20}\log k/\log\log k$, the third term is at most $(\log k)^{-10/9}$. The last term $2r\lambda$ is $o(k^{\theta}/\log k)$, using $r\le\log k$. Since both log exponents are below $-1$, for every $C>0$ the right-hand side is eventually at most $Ck^{\theta}/\log k$.

## Konyagin's theorem {steps: h_kon}

The general Konyagin bound is the theorem of the node `erdos-1094--h2--h2--h2--h3--h1`, which is recorded as a lemma of this node, used as it stands.

## Splitting on BHP {steps: s2 s3}

If $\mathrm{BHP}$ holds, the large-case lemma gives the second disjunct. Given $C,k_b$ from $\mathrm{BHP}$, it takes $k$ beyond $k_b$, beyond the threshold of the choice of $r$ and beyond the asymptotic threshold for this $C$. Then $\#B(k,n)<Ck^{\theta}/\log k\le\pi(k,k+k^{\theta})$, and a prime $p\in(k,k+k^{\theta})$ divides $P_k(n)$. If $\mathrm{BHP}$ fails, the first disjunct holds.
