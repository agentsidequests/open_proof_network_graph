## Overview

Write $\theta=21/40$, $P_k(n)=\prod_{i=1}^{k}(n-i)$, $\|x\|$ for the distance from $x$ to a nearest integer, and $\pi(a,b)$ for the number of primes in $(a,b)$. Let $\mathrm{BHP}$ be: there are $C>0$ and $k_0$ with $C k^{\theta}/\log k\le\pi(k,k+k^{\theta})$ for all $k\ge k_0$. The theorem: $\neg\mathrm{BHP}$, or eventually in $k$, for every integer $n$ with $k^2/\log^2k<n\le\tfrac12k^{2+\theta}$, some prime $p$ with $k<p<k+3k^{\theta}$ divides $P_k(n)$.

The structure is that of the medium case. Let $B(k,n)$ be the integers $m$ with $k<m<k+k^{\theta}$ and $\|n/m\|<k^{\theta-1}$. If $\#B(k,n)<\pi(k,k+k^{\theta})$, some prime $p\in(k,k+k^{\theta})$ has $\|n/p\|\ge k^{\theta-1}$; then $i=n\bmod p$ satisfies $1\le i\le k$, so $p\mid n-i$ and $p\mid P_k(n)$. The lemmas before the theorem prove this step, the asymptotic comparisons $(\log x)^b x^{-c}\to 0$ and their consequences, and the bound on $\#B(k,n)$ below.

**The count on this range.** The input is the node's hole, Konyagin's theorem at a general exponent $r$ and scale $\lambda$. For $\lambda\ge 1$, $k<n$, $2\le r\le\tfrac12k^{1-\theta}$,
$$\#B(k,n)<65536\,k^{\theta}\Bigl[\Bigl(\tfrac{n\,r!\,\lambda^r}{k^{r+1}}\Bigr)^{\frac1{2r-1}}+\Bigl(\tfrac{k^{r+\theta}}{n\,r!\,\lambda^r}\Bigr)^{\frac1{r-1}}+\Bigl(\tfrac{(r+1)\lambda}{k}\Bigr)^{\frac1{2r}}\Bigr]+2r\lambda .$$
The proof applies it with $r=2$ and $\lambda=\lambda(k,n)=\sqrt{k^{2+\theta}/(2n)}\,\log k$. On the range, $\lambda\ge 1$ (from $n\le\tfrac12k^{2+\theta}$ and $\log k\ge 1$ for $k\ge 3$), and $\lambda\le\lambda^+(k)=\tfrac1{\sqrt2}k^{\theta/2}\log^2k$ (from $n>k^2/\log^2k$). Since $n\cdot 2!\cdot\lambda^2=k^{2+\theta}\log^2k$, the first two terms become $(k^{\theta-1}\log^2k)^{1/3}$ and $(\log k)^{-2}$. The third is at most $(3\lambda^+/k)^{1/4}$ and the last is $4\lambda\le 4\lambda^+$. Hence, eventually (once $2\le\tfrac12k^{1-\theta}$, $\log^2k<k$ and $k\ge 3$, which also gives $k<n$),
$$\#B(k,n)<g(k)=65536\,k^{\theta}\Bigl[(k^{\theta-1}\log^2k)^{1/3}+(\log k)^{-2}+(3\lambda^+(k)/k)^{1/4}\Bigr]+4\lambda^+(k).$$

**The comparison.** For each $C>0$, eventually $g(k)\le C k^{\theta}/\log k$. Each of the four terms is eventually at most $\tfrac C4 k^{\theta}/\log k$, because each is a power of $k$ strictly below $k^{\theta}$ times a power of $\log k$, or $k^{\theta}$ times a power of $\log k$ below $-1$: the exponents are $(4\theta-1)/3$, then $\theta$ with $(\log k)^{-2}$, then $\theta+(\theta/2-1)/4$, and $\theta/2$.

## Choosing the thresholds {steps: s3 s4 s5 hkb hki1 hki2 hk2le hk1}

Given $\mathrm{BHP}$ with $C,k_b$, take $k_1$ from the count ($\#B(k,n)<g(k)$ on the range) and $k_2$ from the comparison ($g(k)\le Ck^{\theta}/\log k$). Then $k_0=\max(k_b,k_1,k_2,2)$, and $k\ge k_0$ exceeds each threshold.

## Finishing {steps: hbhp hcard close}

For $k\ge k_0$ and $n$ in the range, $\#B(k,n)<g(k)\le Ck^{\theta}/\log k\le\pi(k,k+k^{\theta})$. So some prime $p\in(k,k+k^{\theta})$ lies outside $B(k,n)$ and divides $P_k(n)$, and $p<k+3k^{\theta}$.
