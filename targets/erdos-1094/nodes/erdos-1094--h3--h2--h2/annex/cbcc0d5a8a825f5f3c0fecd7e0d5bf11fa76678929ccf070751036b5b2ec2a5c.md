Informal argument for erdos-1094--h3--h2--h2 (Erdős–Lacampagne–Selfridge 1993, Theorem 1, middle range), written so that it splits into one deep hole and an elementary count.

Setting. Fix $c > 0$. Let $k$ be large, $k^{1+c} < n < k^{7/4}$, put $y = k^{7/8}$ and $I = (k/2,\ k/2 + y]$. For large $k$ every $p \in I$ has $k < 2p$ and $p < k$, so $k \bmod p = k - p$ and $\lfloor k/p \rfloor = 1$.

Step 1 (Kummer / Lucas). For a prime $p$ with $p \le k < 2p$: if $n \bmod p < k \bmod p$ then $p \mid \binom{n}{k}$. (Lucas: $\binom{n}{k} \equiv \binom{n \bmod p}{k \bmod p}\binom{\lfloor n/p \rfloor}{1} \pmod p$, and the first factor is $0$.) This is the lemma `e1094_dvd_of_mod_lt` of the merged partial on the sibling erdos-1094--h3--h2--h3.

Step 2 (a failing $p$ lies in a short interval). Call $p \in I$ failing if $n \bmod p \ge k - p$. Write $a = \lfloor n/p \rfloor$, so $ap \le n < (a+1)p$. Failing means $n - ap \ge k - p$, i.e. $(a-1)p \le n - k$. Hence a failing $p$ lies in $J_a = \big(n/(a+1),\ (n-k)/(a-1)\big]$.

Step 3 (how many $a$). For $p \in I$, $n/(k/2+y) \le n/p < 2n/k$, so $a$ takes at most $2n/k - n/(k/2+y) + 2 \le 4ny/k^2 + 2$ values.

Step 4 (each $J_a$ is short). $|J_a| = \dfrac{2n - k(a+1)}{a^2 - 1}$. Since $a + 1 > n/p \ge n/(k/2+y)$, the numerator is below $2n - kn/(k/2+y) = 2ny/(k/2+y) < 4ny/k$, and $a \ge n/(k/2+y) - 1 \ge n/k$ for large $k$ (as $n > k^{1+c}$), so $a^2 - 1 \ge n^2/(2k^2)$. Hence $|J_a| \le 8ky/n$, and $J_a$ holds at most $8ky/n + 1$ integers.

Step 5 (count). The failing integers of $I$ number at most $(4ny/k^2 + 2)(8ky/n + 1) = 32y^2/k + 4ny/k^2 + 16ky/n + 2 \le 32k^{3/4} + 4k^{5/8} + 16k^{7/8-c} + 2$, using $n < k^{7/4}$ and $n > k^{1+c}$.

Step 6 (the deep input: Ingham 1937). For $\theta > 5/8$ the interval $(x, x + x^\theta]$ contains $(1 + o(1))\,x^\theta/\log x$ primes. With $x = k/2$, $I$ contains at least $y/(2\log k)$ primes for large $k$, and $k^{7/8}/(2\log k)$ exceeds the bound of Step 5 for large $k$ (every term there is $o(k^{7/8}/\log k)$).

Conclusion. Some prime $p \in I$ is not failing, so $n \bmod p < k - p = k \bmod p$, and by Step 1 $p \mid \binom{n}{k}$, with $k < 2p$ and $p < k$: the node's conclusion.

Suggested Lean split: one hole for Step 6 in natural numbers (for some $C$, $K$ and all $k > K$, a Finset of primes $p$ with $k < 2p$ and $(2p - k)^8 \le 2^8 k^7$ whose card is at least the right-hand side of Step 5 plus one, e.g. stated as $C \cdot (\text{card}) \cdot \log_2 k \ge k^{7/8}$ in an integer form), and Steps 2 to 5 proved as an injection of the failing primes into pairs $(a, j)$ with $a$ in the range of Step 3 and $j$ an index within $J_a$. The real-exponent hypotheses $k^{1+c} < n$ and $n < k^{7/4}$ are only used through $n^4 < k^7$ and $k^{1+c} < n$; the latter enters only to make $16ky/n = o(k^{7/8}/\log k)$.
