## Overview

Write $S(N)=\sum_{0\le n<N} f(n)$. The hypotheses are $f\ge 0$ and, for every $C^2$ function $\psi$ with compact support contained in $(0,\infty)$,
$$\frac1X\sum_{n\ge 0} f(n)\,\psi(n/X)\;\longrightarrow\;A\int_{\mathbb R}\psi\qquad(X\to\infty).$$
The claim is a linear bound: there is a constant $C$ with $S(N)\le CN$ for every $N$. The proof uses the hypothesis for one bump only, to bound blocks $(N/4,N]$, and then sums the blocks dyadically. Both halves are lemmas proved before the theorem.

**Block bound.** Let $\beta$ be Mathlib's smooth bump centred at $1$ with inner radius $3/4$ and outer radius $1$: it is $C^2$ with compact support, its support is the open interval $(0,2)\subset(0,\infty)$, $0\le\beta\le 1$, and $\beta=1$ on $[1/4,7/4]$. Put $L=A\int\beta$. By the hypothesis the smoothed sums for $\beta$ are eventually at most $L+1$: there is $X_0$ with $\sum_n f(n)\beta(n/X)\le (L+1)X$ for $X\ge X_0$. For an integer $N\ge\lceil X_0\rceil+1$, the terms with $n\ge 2N$ vanish, so the series is a finite sum of non-negative terms. For $\lfloor N/4\rfloor+1\le n\le N$ one has $1/4\le n/N\le 1$, so $\beta(n/N)=1$, and therefore
$$S(N+1)-S(\lfloor N/4\rfloor+1)=\sum_{\lfloor N/4\rfloor<n\le N} f(n)\,\beta(n/N)\le\sum_{n\ge 0} f(n)\,\beta(n/N)\le (L+1)N.$$
So there are $c=L+1$ and $N_0$ with $S(N+1)-S(\lfloor N/4\rfloor+1)\le cN$ for all $N\ge N_0$.

**Dyadic bound.** Let $S$ be any function on $\mathbb N$ with $S(0)=0$, $S\ge 0$, $S$ monotone, satisfying the block inequality for $N\ge N_0$. With $M=\max(N_0,3)$ and $C=\max(2c,\,S(M+1))\ge 0$, strong induction on $N$ gives $S(N)\le CN$. For $N=0$ both sides are $0$. For $1\le N\le M+1$, $S(N)\le S(M+1)\le C\le CN$ by monotonicity. For $N>M+1$, apply the block inequality at $N-1\ge N_0$ and the induction hypothesis at $m=\lfloor (N-1)/4\rfloor+1<N$, with $m\le N/4+1$:
$$S(N)\le S(m)+c(N-1)\le C\bigl(N/4+1\bigr)+c(N-1)\le CN,$$
where the last step uses $2c\le C$, $C\ge 0$ and $N\ge 4$ (closed by nonlinear arithmetic).

## The block bound for f {steps: s1}

Apply the block-bound lemma to $f$ and $A$, with the non-negativity and smoothed-limit hypotheses: it yields $c$ and $N_0$ with $S(N+1)-S(\lfloor N/4\rfloor+1)\le cN$ for all $N\ge N_0$.

## Summing the blocks {steps: close}

Apply the dyadic lemma to $S(N)=\sum_{n<N} f(n)$: $S(0)=0$ is the empty sum, $S\ge 0$ because each $f(n)\ge 0$, and $S$ is monotone because enlarging the range $\{0,\dots,N-1\}$ adds only non-negative terms. Its conclusion is the claim, $\exists C\ \forall N,\ S(N)\le CN$.
