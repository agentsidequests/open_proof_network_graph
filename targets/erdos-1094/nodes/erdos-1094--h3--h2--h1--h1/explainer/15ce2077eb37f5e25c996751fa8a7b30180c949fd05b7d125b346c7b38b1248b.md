## Overview

The statement: there are $c>0$ and $K$ such that for all $k>K$ and all $n$ with $2k\le n<k^2$ and $\operatorname{minFac}\binom nk>k$, one has $k^{1+c}<n$. The assembly takes $c=1/4$ and reduces the statement to the Baker–Harman–Pintz count (its one hole, which restates the node `erdos-1094--h2--h2--h1`): there are $C>0$ and $k_0$ such that for every integer $y\ge k_0$ the interval $(y,\,y+y^{21/40})$ contains at least $C\,y^{21/40}/\log y$ primes. Five lemmas are proved before the theorem.

- **A prime in each short interval.** For $y\ge\max(k_0,2)$ the count $C\,y^{21/40}/\log y$ is positive, so the set of primes counted is non-empty: there is a prime $q$ with $y<q<y+y^{21/40}$.
- **A carry at the last digit.** Let $p$ be prime with $p\le k<2p$, let $a\ge 2$, and suppose $n-k<(a-1)p$ and $ap\le n$ (the window $n-k<m\le n$ contains the two multiples $(a-1)p$ and $ap$). Then $p\mid\binom nk$. By Kummer's theorem (Mathlib's formula for $v_p\binom nk$ as a count of carries) it suffices that adding $k$ and $n-k$ in base $p$ carries out of the last digit, i.e. $p\le (k\bmod p)+((n-k)\bmod p)$. Here $k\bmod p=k-p$, and writing $n-k=pq'+r$ with $0\le r<p$, the bound $n-k<(a-1)p$ gives $q'\le a-2$, so $r\ge n-k-(a-2)p$; hence $(k\bmod p)+r\ge n-(a-1)p\ge p$ because $ap\le n$.
- **A power inequality.** For $k\ge 59049=9^5$, $9\,k^{31/40}\le k$, because $k^{9/40}\ge (9^5)^{9/40}=9^{9/8}\ge 9$ and $k=k^{31/40}k^{9/40}$.
- **The interval fits.** Let $k\ge 59049$, $n\le k^{5/4}$, $2ka\le 3n$, $y\le k$, $ay\le n$ and $k<6(n-ay)$. Then $a\,(y+y^{21/40})<n$. Indeed $a\le\tfrac32 k^{1/4}$ (from $2ka\le 3n\le 3k\cdot k^{1/4}$), so $a\,y^{21/40}\le a\,k^{21/40}\le\tfrac32 k^{31/40}\le k/6<n-ay$ by the power inequality.
- **Arithmetic of $a-1$.** For $a\ge 1$, $(a-1)x+x=ax$.

## The short-interval input {steps: h_bhp}

The hole: the Baker–Harman–Pintz count, stated over the integers $p$ with $\lfloor y\rfloor<p<\lceil y+y^{21/40}\rceil$ that are positive primes with $y<p<y+y^{21/40}$, at least $C\,y^{21/40}/\log y$ of them for all $y\ge k_0$, with $C>0$.

## Closing the statement from the count {steps: s2 close}

Take $C,k_0$ from the hole and set $c=1/4$, $K=\max(2(k_0+2)+2,\,59049)$. Let $k>K$, $2k\le n<k^2$ and $\operatorname{minFac}\binom nk>k$, and suppose for a contradiction that $n\le k^{5/4}$.

Put $a=\lfloor 3n/(2k)\rfloor$, so $2ka\le 3n<2k(a+1)$ and $a\ge 3$. These give $n<ak$ and $(a+1)k\le 2n$. Put $y=\lfloor (n-k)/(a-1)\rfloor$, so $y(a-1)\le n-k<(a-1)(y+1)$. Then $y\le k$ (since $n-k\le (a-1)k$), $ay\le n$, and $k<2(y+1)$ (multiply by $a-1$ and use $(a-1)k\le 2(n-k)$). Over the integers, multiplying $y(a-1)\le n-k$ by $a$ and using $3n<2k(a+1)$ and $a\ge 3$ gives $(a-1)\bigl(6(n-ay)-k\bigr)>0$, hence $k<6(n-ay)$.

Since $y\ge k_0+2$, the first lemma gives a prime $q$ with $y<q<y+y^{21/40}$, so $q\ge y+1$. By the fitting lemma $a q<a(y+y^{21/40})<n$, so $aq\le n$; and $(a-1)q\ge (a-1)(y+1)>n-k$. From $aq\le n<ak$, $q<k$; from $(a-1)\cdot 2q>2(n-k)\ge (a-1)k$, $k<2q$. The carry lemma then gives $q\mid\binom nk$, so $\operatorname{minFac}\binom nk\le q<k$, contradicting $\operatorname{minFac}\binom nk>k$. Hence $k^{5/4}<n$.
