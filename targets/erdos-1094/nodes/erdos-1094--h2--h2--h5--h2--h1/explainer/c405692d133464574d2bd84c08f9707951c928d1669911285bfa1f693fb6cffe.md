## Overview

Fix $0<k\le n$ (the real parameter $c>0$ is not used). Suppose that no prime $p$ with $k<p\le\lfloor n/k\rfloor$ divides any integer of the window $n-k<m\le n$. For such a window integer $m$ write
$$s(m)=\prod_{p\le k,\ p\ \text{prime}} p^{v_p(m)}$$
for its $k$-smooth part. The claim is that $s(m)=m$ (the integer $m$ is $k$-smooth) or $s(m)<k$. The proof factors $m=s\cdot r$, where $r$ is the product of the prime powers of $m$ at primes above $k$; the hypothesis forces every prime of $r$ to exceed $n/k$; and then $s\ge k$ together with $r>1$ would make $m=sr$ larger than $n$.

## The factorisation m = s r {steps: hm0 hsplit}

Since $m>n-k\ge 0$, $m\ne 0$. By unique factorisation $m=\prod_{p\mid m} p^{v_p(m)}$. Split this product over the prime factors $p\le k$ and those with $p>k$. The first part equals $s$: the product defining $s$ runs over all primes $p\le k$, and a prime $p\le k$ that does not divide $m$ contributes $p^0=1$. The second part is, by definition, $r=\prod_{p\mid m,\ p>k} p^{v_p(m)}$. Hence $m=s\,r$.

## Either r = 1 or r > n/k {steps: hr1}

If $r\ne 1$, then $r\ne 0$ (a product of positive prime powers), so $r$ has a prime factor $q$. As $q\mid r$ and $r\mid m$, also $q\mid m$. Since $q$ is prime and divides the product defining $r$, it divides one factor $p^{v_p(m)}$ with $p>k$ prime, hence divides $p$, so $q=p>k$. By the hypothesis on the window, $q\le\lfloor n/k\rfloor$ is impossible, so $q>\lfloor n/k\rfloor$; and $q\le r$ because $q\mid r$ and $r>0$. Therefore $r>\lfloor n/k\rfloor$.

## The case r = 1 {steps: s4}

Then $m=s\cdot 1=s$, which is the first alternative.

## The case r > n/k {steps: s5}

Suppose, for a contradiction, that $s\ge k$. Since $r\ge\lfloor n/k\rfloor+1$, we get $m=sr\ge k\,(\lfloor n/k\rfloor+1)$. But $k\,(\lfloor n/k\rfloor+1)>n$ for $k>0$ (division with remainder), so $m>n$, contradicting $m\le n$. Hence $s<k$, the second alternative.
