## Overview

Write $\theta=21/40$, $P_k(n)=\prod_{i=1}^{k}(n-i)$ for $n\in\mathbb Z$, $\|x\|=|x-\mathrm{round}(x)|$ for the distance from $x$ to a nearest integer, and $\pi(a,b)$ for the number of primes $p$ with $a<p<b$. Let $\mathrm{BHP}$ be: there are $C>0$ and $k_0$ with $C\,k^{\theta}/\log k\le\pi(k,k+k^{\theta})$ for all integers $k\ge k_0$. The theorem says: $\neg\mathrm{BHP}$, or there is $k_0$ such that for all $k\ge k_0$ and all integers $n$ with $\tfrac12k^{2-\theta}<n\le k^2/\log^2k$, some prime $p$ with $k<p<k+3k^{\theta}$ divides $P_k(n)$.

The input is the node's hole, Konyagin's count on this range: eventually in $k$, for every such $n$, the set
$$B(k,n)=\bigl\{m\in\mathbb Z:\ k<m<k+k^{\theta},\ \|n/m\|<k^{\theta-1}\bigr\}$$
has fewer than $g(k)=3+56\,k^{2\theta-1}+k^{\theta}(\log k)^{-2}$ elements. Since $g(k)$ is $o(k^{\theta}/\log k)$ while $\mathrm{BHP}$ gives $\gg k^{\theta}/\log k$ primes in $(k,k+k^{\theta})$, some such prime lies outside $B(k,n)$, and a prime $p$ in that interval with $\|n/p\|\ge k^{\theta-1}$ divides $P_k(n)$.

## Konyagin's count {steps: h_count}

The bound on $\#B(k,n)$ above is the theorem of the hole `--h1` of this node, used as it stands (its set is written with the redundant condition $k<m$ repeated inside the filter).

## If BHP fails {steps: s2}

If $\mathrm{BHP}$ fails, the first disjunct holds. Otherwise the proof continues to the second disjunct, with abbreviations for $P_k(n)$, $\theta$ and $\pi(a,b)$.

## Basic facts {steps: theta_pos theta_lt_one dvd_Pprod_of_factor}

$0<\theta<1$; and if $p\mid n-i$ for some $1\le i\le k$, then $p\mid P_k(n)$.

## A prime far from the bad set divides the window product {steps: dvd_from_far exists_far_prime}

Let $k\ge 1$ and let $p$ be a positive integer with $k<p<k+k^{\theta}$ and $\|n/p\|\ge k^{\theta-1}$. Put $i=n\bmod p\in[0,p)$. The fractional part of $n/p$ is $i/p$, so $\|n/p\|=\min(i/p,\,1-i/p)$, and multiplying by $p$ gives $\min(i,\,p-i)\ge p\,k^{\theta-1}>k^{\theta}$ (as $p>k$). Hence $i>k^{\theta}\ge 1$, and $i<p-k^{\theta}<k$. So $1\le i\le k$ and $p\mid n-i$, whence $p\mid P_k(n)$.

Consequently, if $\#B(k,n)<\pi(k,k+k^{\theta})$, there is a prime $p$ with $k<p<k+k^{\theta}$ and $p\mid P_k(n)$: otherwise every prime counted by $\pi(k,k+k^{\theta})$ would lie in $B(k,n)$ (each such prime $p$ satisfies $p<k+\lfloor k^{\theta}\rfloor+2$, and if $p\notin B(k,n)$ then $\|n/p\|\ge k^{\theta-1}$ and the previous paragraph applies), so $\pi(k,k+k^{\theta})\le\#B(k,n)$.

## Asymptotic comparisons {steps: tendsto_log_rpow_mul_rpow_neg poly_log_lt poly_log_lt_eq}

$(\log x)^b x^{-c}\to 0$ for $c>0$; hence for $\alpha<t$ and $C>0$, eventually $A\,k^{\alpha}(\log k)^{\beta}\le C\,k^{t}/\log k$. Also, for any $A$, $t$ and $C>0$, eventually $A\,k^{t}(\log k)^{-2}\le C\,k^{t}/\log k$, because eventually $A\le C\log k$.

## Finishing from the two counts {steps: konyagin_finish med_rhs_le med_card_bound}

If $C\,k^{\theta}/\log k\le\pi(k,k+k^{\theta})$ and $\#B(k,n)<C\,k^{\theta}/\log k$, the previous section gives a prime $p$ with $k<p<k+k^{\theta}\le k+3k^{\theta}$ dividing $P_k(n)$. For each $C>0$, eventually $g(k)\le C\,k^{\theta}/\log k$: each of the three terms of $g$ is eventually at most $\tfrac C3k^{\theta}/\log k$ (the exponents $0$ and $2\theta-1=1/20$ are below $\theta$, and the last term is the case $A=1$, $t=\theta$, $C/3$ of the second comparison). And Konyagin's count, restated with $B(k,n)$, gives $\#B(k,n)<g(k)$ on the range.

## Assembling the medium case {steps: case_medium s15 close}

Given $\mathrm{BHP}$ with $C,k_b$, take $k_0=\max(k_b,k_1,k_2,2)$ with $k_1$ from Konyagin's count and $k_2$ from $g(k)\le C k^{\theta}/\log k$. For $k\ge k_0$ and $n$ in the range, $\#B(k,n)<g(k)\le C k^{\theta}/\log k\le\pi(k,k+k^{\theta})$, so the finishing step gives the required prime. This is the second disjunct.
